The problem

The project started with an observation: when a conversation gets heated, naming what you feel or need can be difficult. I designed for young adults in relationships who want a way to pause and find the words before talking. This audience and the problem were design assumptions to validate.

From sitemap to screen

I mapped three routes from Home: Daily Check-In, Attachment Insights, and Communication Toolkit. Wireframes gave each route a distinct purpose. I kept the check-in within immediate reach and used the menu for secondary options.

Design decisions

  • Start with recognition. Mood and need choices give someone a place to start; a short reflection gives them room to add their own words.
  • Separate immediate and deeper tasks. The check-in, insights, and toolkit are distinct routes. Someone can log how they feel without being asked to interpret attachment patterns or work through a script.
  • Use warmth with restraint. Deep reds and a fingerprint heart convey intimacy. Translucent cards, repeated controls, and simple icons give the visual language a consistent structure. I treated readability and responsiveness as things to protect while translating the design into code.

What the prototype does

The browser prototype links the three routes from Home. Daily Check-In lets visitors select a mood and a need, write a reflection, and save their latest entry in that browser’s local storage. Attachment Insights and Communication Toolkit are concept screens; they don’t analyze entries or generate personalized advice. Try the demo with sample text.

What I’d test next

The hardest part was keeping the warmth of the mockups while building an interface that stayed clear and responsive. Next I’d test whether people can find the check-in, complete it comfortably, and understand where their entry is stored. I’d also review contrast and touch targets before expanding the toolkit. These are next steps, not measured outcomes.
